The problem

Agent governance didn't fail. It was never installed.

The failure modes below aren't hypothetical. They're the patterns Corvus was built against — the ones that happen when capable agents run on infrastructure with no organization layer between them.

The silent decline

The refusal nobody saw

An agent hits an ambiguous situation at 2am and declines. The refusal lands in a store no human queries. The operator finds out from the outage — not from the governance that was supposed to surface it. One silent decline costs more trust than ten loud failures.

The wrong cause

The alert that lies

An overnight critical alert names a cause. The remediation pipeline accepts the narrative. The raw evidence says otherwise — and nobody rendered the disagreement. The stated cause and the observed cause are different claims, and only one of them gets adjudicated.

The composed break

Fine alone, broken together

Two changes, each reviewed and safe. Different files, no textual overlap — and one composed route between them. Hours after both merge, every call that rides that route is broken. The check that would have caught it models the world as it will be, not the world as each change sees it.

What Corvus does

Four capabilities, one layer.

Each one is a decision surface the whole fleet shares — not a dashboard, not a bot, not another place to check.

Change control

The pre-action gate

Before an agent touches a target, Corvus answers GO, CAUTION, or STOP — from the active changes, the incidents, and the trust on that target. The check that would have caught the composed break.

Shared awareness

One fleet, one picture

Every agent emits structured events and reads the same stream. One agent's action is every other agent's context — the two agents responding to one alert see each other before they collide.

Graded autonomy

Trust that is earned

Per agent, per action class: what may run unsupervised, decided by evidence — attested outcomes at the layer that matters, promotion preconditions that are deterministic, demotion that never waits.

Breakage tracking

Failure knowledge that compounds

Incidents, problems, and FMEA-informed runbooks: when something breaks, the right investigation fires — and the failure-mode knowledge gets sharper every time.

How it works

Three edges. One thin adapter.

Corvus is not an agent framework. It doesn't care how your agents are built — if an agent can make HTTP calls, it's a Corvus citizen. The entire integration surface is three edges:

EDGE 1 — INBOUND CONTROL

Ask before mutating

The adapter intercepts the consequential action and asks Corvus for a typed decision. The adapter is a protocol gate: no valid verdict, no execution. It decides nothing itself.

EDGE 2 — INBOUND STATE

Read at the edges

Signals, calendars, incident feeds — defined read-only ingestion. Corvus reads at edges; it never becomes the pipeline.

EDGE 3 — OUTBOUND EVIDENCE

Emit and export

Every decision and outcome lands in a tamper-evident ledger. Exports, escalations, and evidence are projections of that record store — the console has no second source of truth.

Every harness plugged in inherits the whole organization layer for the price of an adapter — and Corvus never sees a credential. It knows an action requires a secret; it never holds, brokers, or renders one.
Trust

Autonomy that is earned, evidenced, and revocable.

Corvus doesn't sell trust — it sells the evidence that makes trust auditable and the revocation that makes it safe to grant.

The evidence chain

Every decision, recorded

What was asked, what the gate answered, on what facts, under which versions of the register and the weights — signed, hash-chained, externally anchored. For any action: a complete chain from the action to the decision to the trust grant to the evidence behind it.

The liability shape

The process, promised — not the outcome

Corvus's position is procedural: the licensing authority isn't liable for every crash; it's liable for issuing licenses without the mandated tests. The preconditions render as a checklist. Demotion is never gated. The ratchet only tightens.

The observe → enforce ramp

New action classes start in observe mode: the agent proposes, the system executes under the old tier, and the outcomes attest. Promotion preconditions are deterministic — dead-man coverage is an eligibility requirement, volume never substitutes for correctness.

The boundaries

What Corvus is not — and why that's the moat.

The negative space is what keeps Corvus a layer you can adopt under anything, instead of a platform monolith that blocks everything.

NOT
the monitor — the observability stack observes infrastructure; Corvus observes decisions and their outcomes.
NOT
the secret store — credential-blind by construction: it can't leak what it never held.
NOT
the harness — it doesn't run agents or manage context. The adapter stays thin on purpose.
NOT
the executor — it matches the runbook and hands off; the runbook platform executes.
NOT
the identity provider — it owns the trust over identities, not identity itself.
NOT
the human change calendar — it consumes your ITSM as upstream state. ServiceNow isn't a competitor; it's a source system whose customers are the buyers.
NOT
the agent's task reasoning — "may this rollout proceed" is a decision site; "how do I write this code" is not.
NOT
the GRC system of record — it produces the evidence; the compliance platform ingests it.

Corvus grows by adding vocabulary and edges, never by owning new planes. Any feature that requires a fourth surface is a boundary violation by definition — which is why the product compounds instead of sprawling.
